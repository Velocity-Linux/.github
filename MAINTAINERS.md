# Maintainers

## Responsibilities

Maintainers are responsible for the health, quality, and direction of their assigned areas. Specific responsibilities include:

- Reviewing and merging pull requests in a timely manner
- Triageing and labeling issues
- Ensuring tests pass and benchmarks are documented for merged changes
- Upholding coding standards and project policies
- Mentoring new contributors
- Participating in release planning for their area
- Communicating changes to the team and community

## Expectations

- Respond to review requests within 3 business days when possible.
- Do not merge your own PRs without at least one other maintainer review, unless explicitly waived for trivial changes.
- Keep your area's documentation accurate and up to date.
- Do not introduce breaking changes without an RFC and Core Team approval.
- Escalate cross-cutting concerns to the Core Team.

## Review Responsibilities

- Reviews must be substantive. Approving without reading is not acceptable.
- Flag performance regressions, security issues, and policy violations.
- Request changes constructively. Explain the reasoning behind requested changes.
- Approve PRs only when you are confident they meet project standards.
- Use review comments to teach, not just to gatekeep.

## Activity Expectations

Maintainers are expected to remain active. Activity includes:

- Reviewing PRs and issues
- Contributing code or documentation
- Participating in discussions

## Inactive Maintainer Policy

Maintainers who do not meet activity expectations for 90 days are considered inactive. The Core Team will:

1. Send a courtesy notice to the maintainer.
2. If no response within 14 days, move the maintainer to an inactive status.
3. Inactive maintainers lose write access. Access may be restored upon request after the maintainer resumes activity.
4. After 180 days of inactivity, maintainer access may be permanently revoked.

This policy ensures that write access reflects current contributors and reduces the risk of unreviewed merges.
